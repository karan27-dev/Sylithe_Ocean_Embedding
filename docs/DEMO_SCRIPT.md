| 0:24–0:52 | Sylithe Ocean Model | Home | Satellites see the ocean's surface every day, everywhere. But the water below, the thermocline and the warm layer that feeds cyclones, is measured by only a few thousand Argo floats. Our model reads five satellite products, compresses them into an embedding, and reconstructs temperature at fifteen depths, from the surface to 1000 metres, with an honest uncertainty. And it is judged by independent Argo floats, not by the model it was trained on. |

| 5:49–6:35 | Cyclone watch · what the satellites saw | Cyclone watch | Below, every satellite input gets the same three panels: the field, its area mean over time, and the input plotted against the predicted temperature at depth. Sea surface temperature is the first condition, 26 °C or warmer. But it cannot tell a thin warm skin from a deep warm layer, which is why the model looks beneath it. Salinity: river and monsoon freshwater in the Bay of Bengal forms a barrier layer that limits how much cold water a storm can mix up. Sea level anomaly: highs mark warm-core eddies with a deep thermocline, the strongest subsurface signal among the inputs. Currents trace the boundary currents and eddies that shape the thermocline, and wind mixes the upper ocean and drives upwelling. Setting the depth to 200 metres shows how much each surface signal still tells at that level. |
| 6:35–7:05 | Cyclone watch · what the model predicts | Cyclone watch | Then the predictions: temperature, uncertainty, cyclone heat potential, the upper 100-metre mean, the 26 °C isotherm and the mixed layer. The heat potential map marks 50 kJ per square centimetre, above which rapid intensification becomes possible. The upper 100-metre mean is what a storm feels after stirring the ocean: below about 26 °C, the mixed water can no longer feed it. And a shallow mixed layer over a sharp thermocline cools fastest under a storm. |
| 7:05–7:26 | Cyclone watch · OCPI, hotspots and rules | Cyclone watch | The Ocean Cyclone Potential Index combines all of this into one number per cell, from low to very high. And the rules are written out in full, so that every flag can be audited. |

| 8:16–8:50 | Validation · benchmark and leaderboard | Validation | The leaderboard: every method, one referee. Our three-model ensemble reaches 0.662 °C RMSE against GLORYS12, and 0.757 °C against Argo. It beats the published Attention 3D U-Net++, retrained on identical data, ridge regression, climatology, and the HYCOM operational model. In the Bay of Bengal and in the deep ocean, the margin over the U-Net is statistically significant. Only GLORYS itself, which assimilates these same floats, scores lower against Argo. |
