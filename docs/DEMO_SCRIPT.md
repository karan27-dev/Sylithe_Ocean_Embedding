| 0:20–2:13 | Who we are · benchmark · research paper | Split screen: benchmark (left) · research paper scrolling, 13 pages (right) | Before the prototype, who we are, and why we are the right team to build this. Team Sylithe builds real models and working products, not slides. Everything you will see today runs live, like an industry product. We have done this before: a canopy height model, a dynamic control-area baseline for carbon projects, land use and land cover classification, and land change detection. Now we bring that experience to the ocean. The Sylithe Ocean Model learns from nineteen years of satellite data, 2005 to 2023, normalised on one grid. It is trained on 2005 to 2021, and tested on 2023, a year it never saw. On the left is our benchmark, against 9,469 independent Argo measurements. On the right is our research paper: the method, the data, the error at every depth, and every RMSE. We beat the most recent published research, the Attention 3D U-Net++ of Wang et al., 2026, retrained on identical data: 5.2 percent lower error in the Bay of Bengal, and 2.2 percent lower in the deep ocean, both statistically significant. We also beat ridge regression, climatology and the HYCOM operational model. Our ensemble reaches 0.662 °C RMSE against GLORYS12, and 0.757 °C against Argo: number one of every competing model. Only one product still ranks above us against Argo: the GLORYS12 reanalysis, which assimilates those same floats. Version two of our model is in development, and that is its target. Our focus is simple: better models for the ocean, and earlier warning of the conditions that drive cyclones. The live model, our GitHub repository and the paper are linked in the description below. The model is live at sylithe-ocean-model.vercel.app. Now, the prototype. |
